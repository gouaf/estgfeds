National Law Enforcement Statistics Webinar (June 2026) Performance Metrics: This event, held on June 3, 2026, saw 377 attendees. The audience was diverse, including 178 government employees (57 from the Department of Justice), 63 representatives from organizations, 33 from educational institutions, and 79 from commercial sectors. Engagement was high, with 67% of attendees staying for more than 60 minutes of the 90-minute session.   Marketing & Outreach: The marketing campaign achieved open rates between 18% and 20% across three emails. Survey Insights: Attendee Feedback: Participants included federal, state, and local government officials, practitioners (law enforcement and attorneys), and academics. Strengths: Attendees valued the information on interactions between police and the public, data on Federal Law Enforcement Deaths in Custody (FDCRP), and the Census of Federal Law Enforcement Officers 2023. The presenters were noted for being enthusiastic and knowledgeable. Areas for Improvement: Suggestions included providing presentation slides in advance or hard copies to help participants follow along. One attendee noted the material was too advanced for grant writing purposes and suggested better-defining how the data can support tribal grants. Technical Issues: At least one participant reported an inability to log in while using a station VPN.

Federal Justice Statistics Program Webinar (June 2026) Performance Metrics: Held on June 25, 2026, this 90-minute webinar had 592 registrations and 308 actual attendees, resulting in a 48% no-show rate. The average attendance time was 62 minutes, and 55% of the audience stayed beyond an hour. Marketing & Outreach: Email marketing maintained a steady open rate of approximately 17% to 18%. Survey Insights: Attendee Feedback: The audience included state and federal government staff, law students, and advocacy groups. Strengths: The diagrams of data linkage processes and the "walkthrough" of the data site were highly praised. International attendees, specifically a law student from Ukraine, found the structured, data-driven approach of the U.S. system to be a valuable benchmark for their studies.   Areas for Improvement: Several participants suggested lengthening the webinar to two hours to allow for more Q&A. Others requested more focus on qualitative data (mixed-methods) to provide context to quantitative findings. Unique Advocacy: One disability advocate provided extensive feedback suggesting that the BJS should collect more "upstream" data—such as reading levels and disability status of incarcerated individuals—to help the public understand and prevent entry into the justice system.

NIBRS Extract Files and LEARCAT Workshop (July 2026) Performance Metrics: This two-hour workshop took place on July 22, 2026. It had 610 registrations and 347 attendees (a 43% no-show rate). This event had the highest engagement level, with an average attendance time of 1 hour and 34 minutes. Marketing & Outreach: Email open rates ranged from 15% to 19%. A survey link sent the following day saw a 37% open rate and 42 responses. Survey Insights: Attendee Feedback: Respondents included state/local government employees, private researchers, and academic professors. Strengths: The case study linking domestic violence and mass murder was highlighted as particularly illuminating. Presenters were lauded for explaining the "logic and nuances" of the system, helping users avoid potential frustrations when pulling data. Areas for Improvement: There was a strong demand for more live, step-by-step demonstrations of the LEARCAT tool. Attendees also requested fact sheets or summaries of key takeaways to share with colleagues. Geographic Considerations: One attendee from Guam noted that the 1:00 PM ET start time required them to attend at 3:00 AM and requested future sessions at times more accessible for their region.
